Hiring

Hiring is the highest-leverage activity for engineering leaders. A great hire multiplies team effectiveness; a bad hire drains it. This guide covers building effective interview processes and making high-quality hiring decisions.

Conducting Effective Interviews

Interview Structure

0-5 min:   Introduction, put candidate at ease
5-10 min:  Set expectations for the interview
10-50 min: Core interview content
50-55 min: Candidate questions
55-60 min: Wrap up, explain next steps

Best Practices

DoDon’t
Put candidate at easeJump straight into questions
Explain format upfrontKeep them guessing
Give hints when stuckLet them struggle silently
Take notes in real-timeRely on memory
Evaluate against rubricCompare to other candidates
Focus on signal, not tricksUse gotcha questions

Bias Mitigation

BiasDescriptionMitigation
Halo effectOne good trait colors allSeparate rubric dimensions
Similar-to-meFavor similar backgroundsStructured questions
RecencyRemember recent answersTake notes throughout
ConfirmationSeek confirming evidenceLook for disconfirming data
AnchoringFirst impression dominatesScore each dimension independently

Debrief and Decision Making

Debrief Structure

1. Independent Write-up (before meeting)
   ├── Each interviewer writes feedback
   ├── Assigns scores per dimension
   └── Overall recommendation

2. Debrief Meeting
   ├── Round-robin: Each interviewer shares
   ├── Focus on evidence, not conclusions
   ├── Discuss discrepancies
   └── Hiring manager makes final call

3. Documentation
   ├── Record decision and rationale
   ├── Store for future reference
   └── Feedback to recruiters

Decision Framework

Strong Yes + Strong Yes = Likely Hire
Strong Yes + Lean Yes = Likely Hire
Lean Yes + Lean Yes = Discuss carefully
Any Strong No = Usually No Hire
Mixed signals = Dig deeper, may need additional interview

Closing Conversation

1. Understand priorities
   "What's most important to you in your next role?"

2. Address concerns
   "What questions or concerns do you have?"

3. Share relevant stories
   "Let me tell you about someone who joined in a similar situation..."

4. Create urgency (honestly)
   "We'd love to have your decision by [date] because..."

5. Make the ask
   "Based on our conversations, I think you'd be a great fit. 
    Are you ready to join us?"

Interview Quick Reference

Common Interview Questions (for you as a leader)

  1. “How do you build an effective interview process?”

    • Define competencies and rubrics
    • Train interviewers on bias
    • Structure interviews consistently
    • Calibrate regularly

  2. “How do you raise the hiring bar?”

    • Clear level expectations
    • Calibration sessions
    • Data on hire quality
    • Hold interviewers accountable

  3. “Tell me about a difficult hiring decision”

    • Use STAR format
    • Show data-driven approach
    • Discuss tradeoffs explicitly
    • Share outcome and learnings

Checklist

  • Job description clear and unbiased?
  • Interview loop covers all competencies?
  • Rubrics defined for each round?
  • Interviewers trained and calibrated?
  • Bias mitigation in place?
  • Debrief process structured?
  • Candidate experience positive?
  • Closing strategy prepared?
