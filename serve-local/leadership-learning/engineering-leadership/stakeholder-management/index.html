Stakeholder Management

Engineering leaders must work effectively with executives, product managers, and cross-functional teams. This guide covers alignment, communication, and influence strategies.

Status Update Framework (SBAR)

S - Situation   → What's happening
B - Background  → Brief context (minimal)
A - Assessment  → Your analysis
R - Recommendation → What you suggest

Example:
"S: Payment processing is down for 15 minutes.
 B: The third-party provider is having issues.
 A: We're losing approximately $10K per hour.
 R: I recommend we switch to backup provider. 
    I need approval to proceed."

The Pyramid Principle

Lead with conclusion, then support with details.

❌ Wrong order:
"We analyzed the data for 2 weeks. We found that
 users in segment A convert better. The p-value
 was 0.02. So we should focus on segment A."

✅ Right order:
"We should focus on segment A because they
 convert 2x better. Here's the data that
 supports this [available if they want details]."

Executive Meeting Prep

Before the Meeting:
├── What's the one thing I need them to understand?
├── What decision or support do I need?
├── What questions will they likely ask?
└── What data supports my points?

During the Meeting:
├── Start with the key point
├── Be concise (they'll ask for more if needed)
├── Own the numbers
└── End with clear next steps

After the Meeting:
├── Send summary email
├── Follow up on commitments
└── Track progress

Tradeoff Conversations

Scenario: PM wants feature by end of quarter

Instead of: "That's impossible."

Try: "Here are the options:
     
     Option A: Full scope, end of Q2
     - All features, proper testing
     - Risk: None
     
     Option B: Reduced scope, end of Q1
     - Core feature only
     - Risk: Missing 2 edge cases
     
     Option C: Full scope, rushed, end of Q1
     - Cut testing, add tech debt
     - Risk: Potential prod issues
     
     I recommend Option B. What matters most?"

Saying No Effectively

Instead of…Try…
”No, we can’t do that""Yes, and here’s what it would take"
"That’s impossible""That’s challenging given [constraint]. Here are alternatives"
"We don’t have time""If we do X, we’d need to drop Y. Which is more important?"
"That’s not how it works""Help me understand the goal. Maybe there’s another way”

Managing Expectations

Estimation and Commitments

Estimation Pitfalls:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ❌ "It'll take 2 weeks"                                    │
│     → Heard as: "It will be done in exactly 2 weeks"        │
│                                                              │
│  ✅ "Our best estimate is 2 weeks, with a range of          │
│      1.5 to 3 weeks depending on [factors]"                 │
│     → Sets realistic expectations                           │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Communicating Delays

As soon as you know:
"I want to flag a potential delay on [project].
 
 Original timeline: March 15
 Current projection: March 22-25
 
 Cause: [specific reason]
 
 What we're doing:
 - [mitigation actions]
 
 Options:
 1. Accept new date
 2. Reduce scope to hit original date
 
 I recommend [X] because [reason]."

Running Effective Meetings

Before Meeting:
├── Clear agenda shared
├── Right people invited
├── Pre-read materials sent
└── Decision-makers identified

During Meeting:
├── Start on time
├── State purpose and desired outcome
├── Facilitate discussion
├── Capture decisions and actions
└── End with clear next steps

After Meeting:
├── Send notes within 24 hours
├── Follow up on action items
└── Track progress

Handling Disagreements

1. Seek to understand
   "Help me understand your concern about X"

2. Find common ground
   "We both want [shared goal]. The question is how."

3. Separate interests from positions
   "What problem are you trying to solve?"

4. Generate options together
   "What if we tried...?"

5. Agree on decision criteria
   "What would need to be true for you to support this?"

6. Escalate constructively if needed
   "Let's bring this to [decision maker] with both perspectives"

1:1 with Your Manager

Come prepared with:
- Progress on key projects (concise)
- Blockers that need their help
- Decisions you need input on
- What you're learning
- Questions about context/strategy

Interview Quick Reference

Common Questions

  1. “Tell me about a time you had to influence without authority”

    • Use STAR format
    • Show building relationships
    • Demonstrate empathy for other perspectives
    • Highlight the outcome

  2. “How do you handle disagreements with your PM?”

    • Start with partnership mindset
    • Seek to understand their goals
    • Present options, not ultimatums
    • Escalate constructively when needed

  3. “How do you communicate with executives?”

    • Bottom line up front
    • Business impact focus
    • Clear asks
    • Concise with details available

Stakeholder Management Checklist

  • Stakeholder map created?
  • Communication cadence established?
  • Expectations aligned?
  • Trust being built through transparency?
  • Conflicts addressed constructively?
  • Managing up effectively?
  • Cross-functional relationships strong?
