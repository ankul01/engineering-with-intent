# Design Reviews That End with Decisions (ADR-Based)

How to run design reviews that produce clear, documented decisions instead of endless discussion cycles.

## Goal

Every design review ends with one of three outcomes:
1. **Approved**: Decision documented, team can proceed
2. **Rejected**: Clear reasons documented, alternative path identified
3. **Needs revision**: Specific gaps identified, next review scheduled

No design review ends with "let's discuss more" without a concrete next step.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Technical design decisions requiring cross-team input
- Architecture changes affecting multiple services
- New technology adoption proposals
- Significant refactoring efforts

**Not in scope:**
- Day-to-day implementation choices within a team
- Bug fixes and small enhancements
- Decisions already made and documented

## Principles

1. **Decisions are first-class artifacts**: Every decision gets an ADR, even if it's "we decided not to do this"
2. **Time-boxed discussion**: 45 minutes max; if not resolvable, it needs more async work first
3. **Pre-read required**: Attendees must read the proposal before the meeting
4. **Disagree and commit**: Once decided, we execute; dissent is documented, not relitigated
5. **Reversibility awareness**: Document whether this decision is one-way or two-way door

## Rituals & Cadence

| Ritual | Frequency | Duration | Participants |
|--------|-----------|----------|--------------|
| Design Review | As needed | 45 min | Author, reviewers (2-4), facilitator |
| ADR Review | Weekly | 30 min | Architecture guild, async-first |
| Decision Retrospective | Quarterly | 60 min | Tech leads, review 3-5 past ADRs |

### Design Review Flow

**Before the meeting (async):**
1. Author writes ADR draft (Context, Options, Recommendation)
2. Author requests review 48h before meeting
3. Reviewers add comments/questions async
4. Author addresses async feedback

**During the meeting (45 min):**
- 0-5 min: Context check (does everyone understand the problem?)
- 5-15 min: Walk through options and trade-offs
- 15-35 min: Discussion of concerns and alternatives
- 35-45 min: Decision time (vote if needed, facilitator calls it)

**After the meeting:**
1. Author updates ADR with decision and rationale
2. ADR merged to repo within 24h
3. Slack notification to relevant channels

## Artifacts

### ADR Template

```markdown
# ADR-NNN: [Title]

## Status
[Proposed | Accepted | Deprecated | Superseded by ADR-XXX]

## Context
[What is the issue that we're seeing that is motivating this decision?]

## Decision
[What is the change that we're proposing and/or doing?]

## Consequences
[What becomes easier? What becomes harder?]

## Options Considered
[Brief summary of alternatives and why they were rejected]
```

### Pre-Read Checklist
- [ ] Problem statement is clear
- [ ] At least 2 options considered
- [ ] Trade-offs explicitly stated
- [ ] Recommendation stated with rationale
- [ ] Reversibility assessed

### Decision Log
Maintain a living index of ADRs:
- By status (active, deprecated, superseded)
- By domain (data, infrastructure, API, etc.)
- By date (most recent first)

## Metrics

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Time from proposal to decision | < 2 weeks | Prevent decision debt |
| ADR completion rate | 100% | Every review produces documentation |
| Pre-read compliance | > 80% | Meetings are productive |
| Decision reversal rate | < 10% | Decisions are well-considered |
| Meeting overrun rate | < 20% | Time discipline maintained |

## Guardrails

1. **No meeting without ADR draft**: Reject review requests without written proposal
2. **No meeting without pre-read time**: Minimum 48h between proposal and meeting
3. **No decision without quorum**: Define required attendees (e.g., domain owner, SRE rep)
4. **No ADR without consequences**: Must document what changes

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- "Architecture by committee" — reviewer count > 5
- "Bike-shedding" — time spent inversely proportional to importance
- "Decision paralysis" — requiring 100% consensus
- "Shadow decisions" — decisions made outside the process

## Incident Handling

**When the process fails:**

| Scenario | Response |
|----------|----------|
| Decision made without ADR | Retroactively document; review process with team |
| Review takes > 3 meetings | Escalate to engineering leadership; scope issue |
| Reviewers don't pre-read | Cancel meeting; require async feedback first |
| Decision frequently reversed | Review decision quality; improve options analysis |

## Migration / Change Management

**Adopting this process in an existing team:**

**Week 1-2:**
- Introduce ADR template
- Document 2-3 recent decisions retroactively
- Share examples of good ADRs

**Week 3-4:**
- Run first formal design review with new process
- Facilitator guides format strictly
- Retrospective on what worked

**Week 5-8:**
- Team runs reviews independently
- Check-in: are meetings ending with decisions?
- Iterate on template and process

**Common adoption challenges:**
- "We don't have time for documentation" → Show time saved from avoided re-discussion
- "This is too formal for our team" → Start lightweight; add structure as needed
- "Decisions keep getting overturned" → Review decision quality, not process
